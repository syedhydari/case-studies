The scholar · 2025 – present Researcher, Quant Finance · Columbia University — Mathematical Finance & Statistics · publication-track under Dr. Mikhail Smirnov

Where should a regime signal enter a Kelly pipeline? A publication-stage answer.

Situation

Kelly allocation is growth-optimal with known parameters and fragile with estimated ones. Regime conditioning is a standard remedy — but the literature leaves its central design choice open: should the regime signal select the estimation data, or scale the exposure?

Task

Answer that question at publication grade: rules fixed before evaluation, every decision out-of-sample, every figure net of transaction costs, and claims parked at exactly the altitude the evidence supports.

Action

First-authored a study built as a controlled contrast — the same online Bayesian changepoint (BOCPD) regime signal used two ways, everything else identical — evaluated under one shared protocol across three universes: a point-in-time, survivorship-controlled S&P 500 large-cap panel (2000–26), a dimensionally matched EuroStoxx 50 mirror (2000–2018), and nine US-sector ETFs (1999–2025), with a frozen, one-shot COVID/2022 holdout committed in advance. Proved a long-only shrinkage identity extending Rising–Wyner (2012); posterior machinery MCMC-verified (R̂ < 1.006).

Result

The answer is consistent everywhere: sizing-side use is robust; data-side use is fragile — the data-side variant ran the deepest drawdown in every universe (−72% to −77%), and its apparent Sharpe edge (~0.77 with contemporaneous labels) collapsed to ~0.03 under a one-week label lag. The pre-specified sizing rule led return-per-unit-of-drawdown (Calmar) in all three universes; on the primary panel it led every risk-adjusted metric — net Sharpe 0.40, CAGR 8.4%, 7.3× terminal wealth vs 2.6× for minimum-variance.

Stated with the paper's own restraint: no Sharpe-dominance claim, no shallowest-drawdown claim — and in the fastest crash, passive diversification wins. Reported as-is.

The builder · 2023 – 2025 Systematic Quant R&D · Delta Strategy Co — pre-AUM research under senior buy-side (ex-Guggenheim) leadership

A pre-AUM research program where process was the product.

Situation

Systematic strategies degrade out of sample for well-understood reasons: non-stationarity, regime change, overfitting, signal decay. A pre-AUM program has no track record to lean on — so its only credible asset is a process an institutional risk committee could audit.

Task

Originate a risk-first research framework and carry it to a deployment-aware standard — the evidence bar vetted upstream of any allocation — rather than an in-sample one.

Action

Originated a multi-variant Bayesian–Kelly prototype — an online (BOCPD) four-state regime classifier paired with pre-specified fractional-Kelly sizing — with dial values and classifier priors locked before evaluation, so every walk-forward decision stayed genuinely out-of-sample. Built the validation program around it: a-priori transaction-cost and slippage modeling charged on turnover, strict point-in-time / no-look-ahead data handling, and Monte Carlo / jump-diffusion stress across Dot-Com, GFC, and Euro-crisis regimes. Owned the research-to-deployment infrastructure: schema-controlled SQL with audit trails, reproducible retrieval, and a decision layer translating regime state into exposure and drawdown thresholds.

Result

A framework whose evaluations could not be tuned after the fact, and a mapped cost envelope showing where edge degrades under frictions — deployability evidence, produced deliberately. The program's directional logic was consolidated into a public executive abstract (2025), and its open questions moved with me to Columbia for formal treatment.

Pre-AUM by design: no outside capital was managed and no performance representation is made — the standards are the result.

The operator · 2020 – 2023 Quant Risk Strategist · Risk Haas — private risk advisory for investment and family-office mandates

When COVID broke a month-end risk process, I rebuilt it as an engine.

Situation

Eight weeks into a private advisory engagement, the COVID crash exposed a spreadsheet-based, month-end risk process: by the time exposures were recomputed and reconciled, the book had moved. De-risking conversations were running on stale numbers, exactly when speed mattered most.

Task

Make risk measurable, governed, and fast — for real client mandates, under real stress.

Action

Built a SQL-based portfolio risk engine recomputing cross-asset exposures and VaR/CVaR in near real time, reconciled against client-side factor and risk reports. Expanded scope to design the practice's enterprise and exposure-risk frameworks — risk taxonomy, KRIs, risk-appetite alignment, ISO 31000-aligned monitoring, and scenario shock propagation across rates, volatility, and correlation. Rebuilt the client risk-reporting pipeline, and delivered volatility-resilient overlays through the 2022 rates shock.

Result

Risk-response latency cut roughly 40% during market shocks. Overlays held sub-1% rolling daily VaR and single-digit drawdown thresholds through the 2022 regime break — a year that flipped the stock–bond correlation and broke conventional diversification. Data-extraction errors in client reporting were materially reduced, shifting reviews from “is this number right?” to “what should we do?”

The machinery · 2018 – 2020 Post-Baccalaureate Research Fellow · UCLA — Bayesian & stochastic neuro-modeling under Dr. Katsushi Arisaka

The methods came from a neuroscience lab — on purpose.

Situation

Neural time series are among the harshest training grounds in statistics: weak signal in heavy noise, non-stationary, regime-switching, artifact-ridden. A lab standard where estimates without uncertainty were treated as malpractice.

Task

Build inference machinery that extracts structure honestly — and prove, rather than assume, that it works.

Action

Developed Bayesian and stochastic inference models on noisy neural time-series data: MCMC with disciplined convergence diagnostics, hierarchical inference, Itô/Langevin dynamics, hidden Markov models, and ARIMA baselines that any richer model had to beat.

Result

Three doctrines that never left: baselines before beliefs; filtering, not smoothing — never let a model peek at the future; and shrinkage as a principle, not a trick. The methods were later adapted to financial regime-detection frameworks — starting with a live systematic book on personal capital, launched mid-fellowship in January 2019 and run for four and a half years.

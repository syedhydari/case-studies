The scholar · 2025 – present Researcher, Quant Finance · Columbia University — Statistics & MAFN · research program on regime information and estimation risk

Should a regime signal choose the estimation data, or size the bet? Is the state worth knowing at all?

Paper 1 · first author, with M. Kishmar Regime information in Kelly allocation under estimation risk: exposure sizing versus sample selection SSRN 7140658 · under review, Quantitative Finance Paper 2 · sole author Sample gating: estimator discontinuity under latent-state conditioning SSRN 7538119 · submitted, The Journal of Risk Paper 3 · sole author When is a state worth knowing? The growth value of conditioning information under estimation risk SSRN 7604562 · preprint

Situation

Kelly allocation is growth-optimal with known parameters and fragile with estimated ones. Regime conditioning is a standard remedy, but the literature leaves its central design choice open: should the regime signal select the estimation data, or scale the exposure? And when it selects the data, a small change in classifier output can replace the entire estimation subsample, so the estimate jumps whenever the inferred state flips.

Task

Answer both questions at publication grade: matched comparisons, a lagged signal, every portfolio figure net of transaction costs, and claims parked at exactly the altitude the evidence supports. I originated and led the program — first author of Paper 1, sole author of Papers 2 and 3 — leading theory, design, data, code, and writing, with version audit histories and decision logs kept throughout.

Action

Paper 1 is a controlled contrast: the same lagged Bayesian changepoint (BOCPD) signal used two ways. RegimeFilter selects same-state observations; RegimeDial keeps the full-window moments and scales exposure. Windows, constraints, and chronology are matched, across two point-in-time equity universes (S&P 500 large-cap, EuroStoxx 50) and a nine-sector US ETF panel. On the theory side, I extended Rising–Wyner (2012) to long-only Kelly: dialing is exactly equivalent to mean shrinkage on interior windows, and takes no more risky exposure when the budget binds.

Paper 2 names the mechanism — sample gating — and splits every estimator update into arrival (new data, state input fixed) and gating (data fixed, state input changed). With data and components fixed, a hard selector jumps by a between-state distance; I proved that posterior averaging of the selector's own components is Lipschitz in the state probabilities and squared-risk optimal under its posterior model. Tested across six estimator classes and against classifier-free benchmarks (exponential weighting, Ledoit–Wolf), with a Holm adjustment on the full-window forecast tests and a tempered posterior that maps the stability–accuracy frontier.

Result

Placement: sizing beat selection on Sharpe, Calmar, and maximum drawdown in all three panels, and the Sharpe and Calmar advantages survived ex-post volatility matching. Mechanism: at hard-selector transitions, median mean and covariance displacements ran ~7× and 15–27× same-date full-window changes, with moving-block bootstrap intervals above one. Repair: posterior averaging cut median transition displacement 72–81% across six estimator classes (pooling at the selected state: 4–37%), cut minimum-variance turnover 23–30%, and brought 20% covariance reviews from 5.6–6.4 to 2.3–3.7 a year; risk-forecast loss fell against the full window in 9 of 9 comparisons. Value: learning a Kelly fraction per regime costs about (J−1)/(2n) of growth per period, whatever the state's frequency; since 1971, full-Kelly hard conditioning lost to pooling for every label set.

Stated with the papers' own restraint: drawdown-gap intervals exclude zero in two panels and Calmar-gap intervals in none, and single-panel Sharpe tests are underpowered. Against de-levered full-window controls, the incremental drawdown benefit of state timing is unresolved. Classifier-free exponential weighting forecasts risk better than either average. Paper 3's evidence that the labels carry information at all is weak. Reported as-is.

What the papers claim

Stated at the altitude the evidence supports.

  • P1Sizing beats selection on Sharpe, Calmar, and maximum drawdown in all three panels, under matched windows, constraints, and chronology, net of costs.
  • P1The Sharpe and Calmar advantages survive ex-post volatility matching, and hard selection alone trails plug-in Kelly in every panel.
  • P1Dialing is exactly mean shrinkage on interior windows, and takes no more risky exposure when the budget binds.
  • P2With data and components fixed, a hard selector jumps by a between-state distance; averaging the same components is Lipschitz in the state probabilities.
  • P2Averaging cuts transition displacement 72–81% across six estimator classes and lowers risk-forecast loss against the full window in 9 of 9 comparisons.

What they do not

Volunteered before it is asked for.

  • P1Per-panel significance. Drawdown-gap intervals exclude zero in two panels, Calmar-gap intervals in none, and single-panel Sharpe tests are underpowered.
  • P1Value from state timing itself. Against de-levered full-window controls, its incremental drawdown benefit is unresolved.
  • P2A better forecaster than classifier-free smoothing. Exponential weighting beats both averages in all nine comparisons.
  • P2Uniform significance. The plain average's forecast gains are significant in 7 of 9 nominally and 3 after Holm adjustment (the pooled average keeps all 9), and the pooled selector beats its own average significantly in 4 of 9 nominal tests.
  • P2Stability for free. Averaging trades rare large revisions for frequent small ones.

The boundaries are load-bearing. Paper 1 says where the signal should enter; Paper 2 explains why the other entry point breaks, and says plainly where its repair stops beating simpler tools.

The builder · 2023 – 2025 Systematic Quant R&D · Delta Strategy Co — pre-AUM research under senior buy-side (ex-Guggenheim) leadership

A pre-AUM research program where process was the product.

Situation

Systematic strategies degrade out of sample for well-understood reasons: non-stationarity, regime change, overfitting, signal decay. A pre-AUM program has no track record to lean on — so its only credible asset is a process an institutional risk committee could audit.

Task

Originate a risk-first research framework and carry it to a deployment-aware standard — the evidence bar vetted upstream of any allocation — rather than an in-sample one.

Action

Originated a multi-variant Bayesian–Kelly prototype — an online (BOCPD) four-state regime classifier paired with pre-specified fractional-Kelly sizing — with dial values and classifier priors locked before evaluation, so every walk-forward decision stayed genuinely out-of-sample. Built the validation program around it: a-priori transaction-cost and slippage modeling charged on turnover, strict point-in-time / no-look-ahead data handling, and Monte Carlo / jump-diffusion stress across Dot-Com, GFC, and Euro-crisis regimes. Owned the research-to-deployment infrastructure: schema-controlled SQL with audit trails, reproducible retrieval, and a decision layer translating regime state into exposure and drawdown thresholds.

Result

A framework whose evaluations could not be tuned after the fact, and a mapped cost envelope showing where edge degrades under frictions — deployability evidence, produced deliberately. The program's directional logic was consolidated into a public executive abstract (2025), and its open questions moved with me to Columbia for formal treatment.

Pre-AUM by design: no outside capital was managed and no performance representation is made — the standards are the result.

  • hazard rate · classifier priors

The line is the same one the program's public abstract draws. Architecture travels; parameters do not — and how someone handles that boundary with a former engagement is a reasonable forecast of how they will handle it with yours.

The operator · 2020 – 2023 Quant Risk Strategist · Risk Haas — private risk advisory for investment and family-office mandates

When COVID broke a month-end risk process, I rebuilt it as an engine.

Situation

Eight weeks into a private advisory engagement, the COVID crash exposed a spreadsheet-based, month-end risk process: by the time exposures were recomputed and reconciled, the book had moved. De-risking conversations were running on stale numbers, exactly when speed mattered most.

Task

Make risk measurable, governed, and fast — for real client mandates, under real stress.

Action

Built a SQL-based portfolio risk engine recomputing cross-asset exposures and VaR/CVaR in near real time, reconciled against client-side factor and risk reports. Designed the practice's enterprise and exposure-risk frameworks — risk taxonomy, KRIs rationalized 20 → 11 with coverage retained, risk-appetite alignment, ISO 31000-aligned monitoring, and scenario shock propagation across rates, volatility, and correlation. Rebuilt the client risk-reporting pipeline, and delivered volatility-resilient overlays through the 2022 rates shock.

Result

Shock-to-decision cycle cut from ~14.5 to ~8.5 days — roughly 40% — measured on event logs with identical definitions before and after. Overlays held rolling 1-day 99% VaR under 1% of NAV and drawdowns inside an 8% appetite band through the 2022 regime break — a year that flipped the stock–bond correlation and broke conventional diversification. Audited data-extraction errors in client reporting fell from 9% to 3% under an unchanged sampling protocol, shifting reviews from “is this number right?” to “what should we do?”

Published: a practitioner book on risk modeling (2022).

The machinery · 2018 – 2020 Post-Baccalaureate Research Fellow · UCLA — Bayesian & stochastic neuro-modeling under Dr. Katsushi Arisaka

The methods came from a neuroscience lab — on purpose.

Situation

Neural time series are among the harshest training grounds in statistics: weak signal in heavy noise, non-stationary, regime-switching, artifact-ridden. A lab standard where estimates without uncertainty were treated as malpractice.

Task

Build inference machinery that extracts structure honestly — and prove, rather than assume, that it works.

Action

Developed Bayesian and stochastic inference models on noisy neural time-series data: MCMC with disciplined convergence diagnostics, hierarchical inference, Itô/Langevin dynamics, hidden Markov models, and ARIMA baselines that any richer model had to beat.

Result

Three doctrines that never left: baselines before beliefs; filtering, not smoothing — never let a model peek at the future; and shrinkage as a principle, not a trick. The methods were later adapted to financial regime-detection frameworks, and carried into every chapter that follows.
